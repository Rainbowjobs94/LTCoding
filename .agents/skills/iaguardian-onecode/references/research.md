# Research and Experimental Systems

## Unicode and Glyph Formats

Use versioned schemas, ASCII authorization identifiers, explicit label registries,
bounded depth and size, duplicate-key rejection, Unicode normalization rules,
bidirectional-control detection, spoofing checks, and accessible plain-text labels.

Display labels never grant executable meaning. Never use arbitrary evaluation.

## Cryptography and Provenance

Separate hashing, signing, encryption, watermarking, and public registration.
Use maintained libraries and established algorithms. Define canonicalization,
signature context, key trust, rotation, revocation, compromise, and recovery.

A signed manifest can support integrity and attribution to a key. It does not
alone prove authorship, ownership, license, physical truth, or legal rights.

## Blockchain Experiments

Start with bounded local tests and testnets. Define replay resistance, access
control, upgrade boundaries, signer revocation, dispute limitations, and privacy.
Never put confidential sources, private evidence, or secrets on-chain.

Do not claim that a new notation, font, timing mechanism, hash chain, or contract
overrides chain consensus, creates cryptographic strength by obscurity, or
guarantees economic value.

## External Systems

Treat hypervisors, bootloaders, GPU workloads, Electron clients, private
networking, DNS, and container packaging as independent capability profiles.
Verify hardware, licensing, privileges, recovery access, and host support before
planning execution. A Replit web application is not a hypervisor or firmware host.

## Experiment Log

Record hypothesis, baseline, prototype, benchmark, threat model, result,
limitations, replication steps, and next experiment. Use measured outcomes rather
than invented completion or success probabilities.
